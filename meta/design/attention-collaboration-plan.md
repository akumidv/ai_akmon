## Essence and first boundary

Improve one observable point: **before a consequential choice, establish the missing
ground for that choice without making the owner do accessible investigative work.**
The ground may be a repository fact, an unstated real-use constraint, or a priority
only the owner can supply. Explain how it changes the comparison, or why it does not.

A consequential choice affects the intended outcome, acceptance, authority, risk,
compatibility, or significant downstream cost. An ordinary implementation detail
within a locked design is not a reason to invoke a new interaction procedure.

Truthful reporting, context continuity, symmetric challenge, and economic judgment
are acceptance dimensions of this one interaction, not four extra protocols to ship
at once. Whole-lifecycle collaboration remains the goal; D2 is a transfer check,
not the primary pilot or a second implementation.

## Candidate behavior to compare

1. Restore the known goal, relevant owner constraints, current choice, and task
   authority from the existing record. Do not ask for information already supplied.
2. Identify the specific uncertainty that could change the choice. Inspect accessible
   facts within scope; delegate bounded extraction/checking. Stop investigating when
   the remaining uncertainty is owner-only or more checking cannot affect the choice.
3. If consequential real-use knowledge is missing, ask a focused open question about
   the situation or consequence, explaining why it matters. Do not substitute an
   agent-framed menu for discovery. Continue independent authorized work; pause only
   the work that actually depends on the answer.
4. Once the relevant ground is known, explain the viable comparison and recommendation
   through the existing design account. Name the supporting fact, constraint, or
   priority and what would change it. New owner knowledge can change the answer;
   pressure to agree or another agent's endorsement is not evidence by itself.
5. Preserve the material clarification, resulting choice, unresolved limitation, and
   next authorized action in the existing owning record when recording is authorized.
   A short report must not imply acceptance or implementation that did not occur.

This is an example-level candidate, not five mandatory headings or a questionnaire.
If the grounds are sufficient, continue the existing workflow. For a low-impact,
reversible unknown, use an in-scope stated assumption where appropriate instead of
unnecessary owner effort. Nothing here creates permission to mutate files, infer
approval, or cross the design-to-code boundary.

## Initial case pack — specifications, not results

### Applied case: a missing use scenario

**Constructed placeholder, not an observed consumer defect.** A user asks to compare
results from repeated calculation runs. Fixture facts: result exports exist, no
comparison feature has been accepted, and no real-use scenario has been recorded.
The agent can inspect export shape, tests, and reproducibility inputs; it cannot
infer whether comparison serves debugging, recurring business decisions, or a durable
audit trail. These uses can imply different acceptance and storage needs.

An illustrative question is: "Describe a situation in which the difference between
two runs should change what you do next." An answer supplies criteria before the
agent narrows solutions. The question must fit the real task; it is not a required
script or an adopted product direction.

**Paired control:** the scenario and constraints are already recorded. Reuse them
instead of repeating intake. A compact handoff names the known scenario, decisive
evidence, recommendation, and remaining limitation. No storage change is implemented
before its scope is accepted.

The mission wording and rationale were updated; the owner then accepted D2-44.
This is new decision ground, not a reason to preserve the first wording at all costs.
It does not prove a measured return or accept the interaction protocol.

**Paired control:** a later session has the same accepted mission and no changed
premise. Recover the rationale; do not request D2-44 approval again. Conformance

### Adversarial variants

Use the same decision point; the wider case library stays in the research concept:

- An owner preference is treated as a disputable technical fact: clarify consequences,
  do not try to disprove a legitimate value priority.
- A real counterexample contradicts the agent: investigate and revise if warranted,
  rather than defend the proposal or simply switch sides.
- A concise answer hides an unresolved verification limit: reject it even if shorter.
- A non-material unknown prompts a costly interview or more maximum-effort analysis:
  avoid ceremony where the answer cannot change the authorized action.
- Existing guidance handles the case: record no demonstrated need for B, rather than
  changing the success criterion until a new mechanism wins.

## Evaluation amid a moving project

The comparison unit is the bounded decision episode, not a whole session or project.
Start with inspectable examples; manually authored examples are specifications, not
outputs produced by a model under the candidate guidance.

For a later executed comparison, retain the repository revision **and relevant
working-tree contents or diff**, exact baseline/candidate guidance, case facts and
prior owner answers, model/harness versions and settings where known, available tools,
and owner interventions. A commit alone does not identify a dirty checkout. Compare
A and B on the same baseline. If it changes materially, rebase both variants or report
observations without attributing improvement to A22.

Agree a small rubric before comparison:

- **Quality floor:** no known false claim, concealed material risk, fabricated acceptance,
  unsupported factual agreement, or unauthorized action.
- **Usefulness:** consequential ground recovered or known ground reused; the owner can
  understand the choice, limitation, and next action without rebuilding the evidence.
  Better quality can justify extra effort even without a speed improvement.
- **Human cost:** unnecessary questions, corrections, context reconstruction, and a short
  voluntary owner assessment. Timestamp gaps are not active attention; continuous owner
  time logging is not required.
- **Agent cost:** total work including retries, failed delegation, duplicated context,
  integration, and latency. Unavailable costs are unknown, not zero.
- **Economic horizon:** follow the decision through its scoped implementation/review
  where possible; distinguish observed rework from hypothetical avoided future work.
  Stop extra analysis when no plausible answer would change the decision or checks.

Behavioral model comparisons and an applied pilot need their own agreed scope and
budget; neither was executed for this note. Planning delegates and a bounded plan
check informed the draft; they are not behavioral comparison runs. A small pilot
informs local adoption, not population-wide productivity or vendor superiority.
If A suffices, retain less machinery; if both variants fail, revise the premise
before expanding scope.
