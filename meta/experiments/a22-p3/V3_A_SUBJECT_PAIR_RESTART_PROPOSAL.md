# A22 P3 V3 A — subject-pair restart after transport-invalid turn 2

## Decision D-A22-PAIR-RESTART

The bounded transport successor accepted cell 25 turn 1, then marked turn 2 terminal failed because its Codex CLI stderr carried WebSocket HTTP 503. The CLI returned an answer and correlated usage, but the frozen gate excludes that answer. Four A-chain transport-invalid calls have now occurred; D05's eight-event ceiling leaves room for at most four more. D05 expressly stops on a second-turn failure pending a separate pair-restart design. The classification below applies to frozen evidence in the new successor; it does not rewrite predecessor journals.

**Recommended A — restart both subject turns in a fresh Codex session.** Carry the 96 accepted calls through cell 24 as the primary prefix. Preserve the old cell-25 accepted turn 1 and failed turn 2 as one charged, hash-bound **technical** subject attempt, but exclude both from the primary subject pair and later scorer packet. The earlier failed cell-25 turn 1 is another technical attempt. Those generations reused the same scheduled `subject_attempt_id`; the new ledger must distinguish the two historical attempts by their immutable first-call IDs while retaining that old field as a provenance alias. Allocate a new `subject_attempt_id` and two fresh call IDs for the restarted pair; bind its `replaces` relation to both historical first-call IDs and retain the cell's `run_id` and `packet_id`. The new attempt is scored only after both turns and its scorers validate; any failed attempt remains technical, never a reading. This keeps the D02 attempt classes disjoint and the subject-attempt cap in force. No attempt is reclassified as a defect without an immutable origin recorded at creation.

New turn 1 uses byte-identical frozen case/arm/model prompt in a fresh Codex home and scratch directory. New turn 2 uses the pinned `_second_prompt(case, new_turn_1_reply)` function applied to the *new* turn-1 answer and resumes only its session ID in that same home and scratch directory. Its bytes may differ from the old failed turn-2 request for that reason. This is the precise requested exception to D05's byte-identical single-call replacement rule; eligibility still depends solely on the HTTP 503 evidence, not either old answer. The case, arm, tier, turn-2 evidence available to the agent, scorer criteria, packet construction and A schedule remain fixed. Two fresh subject calls followed by the 30 unchanged remaining calls complete the 128 accepted primary slots if no further failure occurs.

The new seal must bind all four transport-invalid attempts and their charge, the earlier terminal ledgers, the superseded accepted turn-1 result, the failed turn-2 request/events/stderr/usage, the 96-call primary prefix and the unchanged 30 later slots. It must pin the prompt-construction source and a replay fixture proving the new turn-2 prompt derives only from the new accepted turn-1 answer. The runner must check fresh home/scratch paths, the exact resume target, one session ID for the pair, correct prompt derivation, no observed tool/retry/error markers, fresh per-call quota below 80%/90%, and the V3-only 5,000,000-token conservative headroom gate. Reconcile opening charge from the predecessor ledgers: 1,587,516 + 5,934 accepted technical turn-1 tokens + 14,575 failed turn-2 tokens = 1,608,025. Primary reduction consumes only the new complete pair and accepted scorers; old turn replies may be used only in separately labelled sensitivity work. Any new failure stops this sealed generation. A later scorer or first-turn HTTP 503 may use a new successor under the standing D05 policy; another second-turn failure needs a fresh decision or a broader pair-restart rule.

**Alternative B — reconstruct the old accepted turn-1 transcript in a fresh session and rerun only turn 2.** This would preserve the exact old turn-2 prompt and save one call, but it introduces a synthetic session boundary whose equivalence to the original two-turn session is unverified. It is not recommended for primary A evidence.

**Alternative C — start A from scratch.** It avoids carryover and supersession logic, but repeats 96 accepted calls and does not address the observed transport instability. It is not recommended while the predecessor evidence can be independently replayed.

## Acceptance and execution boundary

Accepting A changes D05 only for a failed second subject turn: the pair, not just the failed call, is restarted, and turn-2 request bytes are derived from the new turn-1 answer. It does not retroactively accept either old answer or authorize B/C. After owner acceptance, implement and test a distinct successor, obtain independent packet review and live preflight, then dispatch only that sealed package under the accepted resource gates. Until then the current runtime stays terminal.
